You already did the hard part. You recorded a 45-minute episode, said something genuinely useful, and published it. Then 200 people listened, and the other 7.9 billion never found out it existed.

That's the podcast discovery problem in one sentence: the best content on the internet is buried inside long-form audio nobody scrolls to. Short-form video is the distribution engine that fixes it — one episode, cut right, becomes 10 to 15 standalone clips that work on Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Each clip is a new doorway into your show.

Our podcast to shorts editing service does the cutting for you. You keep recording; we turn every episode into a month of short-form content.

Why podcasts need shorts (the numbers-free version)

Nobody needs a stat to see what's happening on their own phone. Open any feed and count how many 40-minute podcast episodes you watch versus how many 30-second clips. The clip is the trailer; the episode is the movie. Trailers are what sell tickets.

A single strong clip does three jobs at once: it entertains on its own (so the algorithm pushes it), it demonstrates your thinking (so strangers start trusting you), and it points back to the full episode (so listeners compound). Podcasters who clip consistently don't just get more views — they get a flywheel where every episode markets the next ten.

The catch is that clipping is a different skill from podcasting. Finding the moment, cutting it to survive without context, captioning it so it works on mute, pacing it so nobody swipes — that's editing work, and it's roughly an hour of work per finished clip when done properly.

Our process: from raw episode to finished clips

1. We find the moments. We listen through your episode (or work from your timestamps if you send them) and flag the segments that stand alone: hot takes, stories, frameworks, quotable lines, contrarian opinions, funny exchanges. A typical 45-minute episode yields 10–15 clip-worthy moments. Not every minute deserves a clip — we'd rather give you 10 strong ones than 20 weak ones.

2. We cut hook-first. Every clip opens with its strongest 3 seconds. If the punchline was at minute 12, the clip starts there and we backfill context in captions. This is the single biggest difference between clips that get watched and clips that get swiped: professional short-form video editing always leads with the payoff.

3. We caption everything. Styled, word-by-word animated captions, synced to your speech. Most short-form viewing starts muted, and captions are what convert a scroller into a watcher. We also add lower-thirds with your name and show title, so every clip brands your podcast even when it's reposted.

4. We finish for every feed. 9:16 masters for Reels, TikTok, and Shorts, with safe-zone framing so captions and faces never get covered by platform UI. Square or landscape versions on request for LinkedIn and X.

What makes a podcast clip actually go viral

No service can promise virality — anyone who does is selling you something. But clips that break out share a pattern, and we cut toward it deliberately:

  • One idea per clip. The moment a clip tries to make two points, retention collapses. We isolate the single strongest beat.
  • The hook is a claim, not a setup. "The reason most podcasts fail" beats "so in today's episode we discuss..." Every clip opens mid-thought.
  • Visual energy. Static waveforms don't hold attention. We add punch-in zooms, speaker framing changes, and b-roll or kinetic text on key phrases. If you record video (even a webcam), we use it — clips with faces outperform audio-only every time.
  • Length discipline. 21–34 seconds is the sweet spot for most podcast clips. Long enough for one idea, short enough to loop. Loops are free replays, and replays are what the algorithm reads as "show this to more people."

What to send us

Getting started takes about two minutes. Send a link to your episode — Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube, a Drive file, whatever you have. Audio-only is fine; if you record video, send that instead, because clips with faces consistently outperform audio-only.

Optional but helpful: your timestamps. If you already know which moments were strong, mark them and we'll prioritize those — though our moment-finding pass catches plenty you'll have forgotten. Also tell us your show's name and any branding (colors, fonts) so lower-thirds match your existing look.

That's it. No briefs, no calls, no onboarding decks. First-time clients usually get their sample clip back within 48 hours and wonder why they waited so long to start clipping.
