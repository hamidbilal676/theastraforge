## What AI Actually Does to Your Footage

"AI editing" isn't a filter — it's a full post-production pass. Here's what happens to your raw clips:

### Cinematic color and cleanup
AI color grading matches every clip so the footage looks consistent — no more jarring shifts between the bright living room and the dim hallway. AI upscaling sharpens phone footage, and noise reduction cleans up grainy interiors.

### Sky replacement and scene enhancement
Gray day? AI swaps in a blue sky with natural-looking clouds on exterior shots. Dull backyard? Enhanced greens. This alone makes a shocking difference in curb-appeal shots.

### Smart pacing and cuts
AI-assisted editing finds the best moments, trims dead air, and paces the walkthrough like a story — exterior hook, grand entrance, kitchen money-shot, bedrooms, backyard payoff. Viewers stay to the end instead of swiping at second four.

### Captions, callouts, and branding
Animated text overlays highlight what matters: "3BD / 2BA — $485,000," "Chef's kitchen with quartz counters," your name, brokerage, and phone number. Captions are non-negotiable — most viewers watch with sound off.

### Multi-format delivery
One shoot becomes three assets: widescreen for MLS/YouTube, vertical for Reels/TikTok/Shorts, square for feed posts. Each is reframed (not just cropped) so nothing important gets cut.

## How to Film for the Best AI Edit (10 Minutes, No Crew)

The edit can only be as good as the footage. But "good footage" doesn't mean professional — it means *usable*. Follow this:

1. **Shoot horizontal, slow, and steady.** Walk slowly. Let the phone's stabilization work. Fast pans make viewers dizzy.
2. **Hit every room in order.** Front exterior → entry → living → kitchen → bedrooms → baths → backyard. The editor will sequence it.
3. **Hold each shot 5–8 seconds.** Longer than feels natural. Short clips can't be slowed down; long clips can always be trimmed.
4. **Open the blinds, turn on every light.** Bright footage grades beautifully; dark footage fights the AI.
5. **Grab 3–5 detail close-ups.** Faucet, backsplash, fireplace, view from the window. These become the texture shots that make edits feel premium.
6. **Film one 15-second "hook" outside.** You on camera: "This $485K listing just hit the market and the kitchen alone is worth the tour." Talking-head hooks crush on Reels.

That's it. Fifteen minutes, one phone, no gimbal required.

## 5 Listing-Reel Formats That Get Inquiries

Don't post the same video everywhere. Ask your editor for:

1. **The cinematic tour (60–90s, 16:9):** MLS, website, YouTube. The full walkthrough.
2. **The hook reel (15–30s, 9:16):** Instagram/TikTok. Opens with the most dramatic shot or you on camera. Built to stop thumbs.
3. **The detail carousel-video (30s):** Close-ups with price callouts. Great for "just listed" announcements.
4. **The neighborhood cut (30–45s):** Streets, parks, coffee shops, schools. Sells the lifestyle around the address.
5. **The before/after or twilight cut:** AI-enhanced dusk shots make any home look like a magazine cover. Post it; watch the saves roll in.
