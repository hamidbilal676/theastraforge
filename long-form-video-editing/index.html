Why long videos die in the first minute

YouTube's own data is blunt: a large share of viewers decide in the first 30 seconds, and the retention curve of an unedited long video falls off a cliff. Raw footage has slow starts, buried hooks and dead stretches — and at 20 minutes long, even a good video has a dozen places where a viewer's thumb drifts to the sidebar.

Retention editing fixes the curve, not just the file. We restructure the opening so the promise lands immediately, cut every stretch that isn't earning its seconds, and place pattern interrupts — b-roll, graphics, zoom changes, chapter beats — where attention naturally dips. The content is yours; the shape is engineered.

That's the difference between a video that gets suggested and one that gets buried, and it compounds: better retention means more watch time, more sessions, and a catalog that keeps recommending itself. Channels running full funnels pair this with our YouTube automation service or cut each long video down with podcast to shorts and short-form editing.
